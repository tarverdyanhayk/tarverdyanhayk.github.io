About

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at University College Dublin. My research studies how households form expectations about inflation, and how those expectations respond to news and monetary policy, using the ECB Consumer Expectations and BOP-HH Surveys.

Before my PhD, I spent nearly a decade in the Economic Research Department of the Central Bank of Armenia, working on monetary and macroprudential policy. I was a visiting scholar at Harvard University in 2018 and completed the Advanced Studies Program at the Kiel Institute for the World Economy.

Research

Work in progress

News, Narratives, and Household Expectations

This paper studies the effects of bank capital requirements on lending and risk-taking. It exploits a unique policy in Armenia that required banks to increase nominal capital rather than capital ratios.

The Effects of Capital Regulation on Bank Lending: Evidence from Armenia

Builds daily measures of German-language news volume, sentiment, uncertainty and economic narratives, and links them to household expectations around ECB policy announcements.

The Inflation Attention Threshold in the Euro Area

Estimates the level of inflation at which euro-area households start paying attention, using the ECB Consumer Expectations Survey, and shows that the attention regime shapes how supply shocks pass through to inflation.

When Banks Actually Raise Equity

with Christopher Palmer, Sergey Sarkisyan, and Aleksander Shirkhanyan

We study bank and firm responses to Armenia’s institution of an absolute bank-equity floor, which forced banks below the threshold to raise equity and curtailed incentives to shrink assets
